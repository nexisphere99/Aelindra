### NPC_D0_001: Servant Brin   Last Breakfast
#### [Passage: `day0_npc_brin`]
#### Trigger: Player interacts with "Breakfast Tray" or "Servant" in chambers

The knock comes at the usual time. Brin with the tray   bread, cheese, cold meat, the not-quite-tea. He enters with marginally more confidence than three days ago. His livery still doesn't fit. His hair still stands up on one side.

"Morning, Your Highness. Marta sent the fresh loaves today   she said you'd want them. Also cheese pastries. She's been baking since midnight and I think the kitchen staff are staging an intervention."

He sets the tray down. The cheese pastries are there   golden, steaming faintly, the smell of rosemary and melted cheese cutting through every other scent in the room. Marta. Even now. Especially now.

"Brin."

"Your Highness?"

I look at him. Sixteen. Sandy hair. Too-big livery. A boy from the tanner's family in the Lower Market who got a palace job because the crown offers patronage to common families. He's been bringing my breakfast for three weeks. He knows how I take my bread   stale, if possible. He knows I eat standing at the window. He remembers the pastry compliment and relayed it to Marta and it made her day.

In three days, a woman will be sitting in this room. Brin will bring her breakfast. He'll be confused. He'll adapt   servants always adapt, it's the job   but the prince he's been learning to serve will be gone, replaced by a princess he doesn't know, and the small, specific intimacy of remembering someone's breakfast preferences will have to be rebuilt from scratch.

"Thank you," I say. It comes out weighted   too much meaning for two words, too much goodbye for a boy who doesn't know it's a goodbye.

He blinks. "You're welcome, Your Highness. Are you... is everything alright?"

"Everything's fine. The pastries are perfect."

He smiles. He leaves. The door closes. The pastries are, in fact, perfect. I eat two of them standing at the window, looking down at the courtyard, and the cheese is hot and the rosemary is sharp and the last breakfast I eat as a man tastes like a childhood I didn't appreciate until it was over.

**[Energy +10, Stress -3, Reputation (Staff) +1]**

### NPC_D0_002: The Sword on the Wall
#### [Passage: `day0_npc_sword`]
#### Trigger: Player examines "Training Sword" in chambers

The training sword. Mounted above the desk on two iron pegs. Blade dull   it was never meant to be sharp. The grip worn to a polish by years of evening drills, the leather wrapping smoothed by palm sweat and repetition until it's as soft as skin.

I take it down. Hold it. The weight is familiar   not heavy, not light, exactly what a practice sword should weigh. The balance point is six inches above the guard, where my index finger naturally rests when I grip for a thrust.

I assume first position. Feet apart, sword extended, left hand behind my back. The stance is muscle memory   I don't think about it. My body simply knows where every part of itself should be in relation to the blade.

I hold it for a long moment. Then I lower the sword. Set it on the desk.

This body knows how to fight. The new body won't   not immediately. The muscle memory will be scrambled, the leverage different, the reach shorter by five inches. I'll have to relearn everything. Aldric will have to reteach me from the beginning, adjusting for a body that is lighter, shorter, with a different centre of gravity and narrower shoulders and 

Breasts. I'll have breasts. How do you hold a sword with breasts? Is there a stance adjustment? Do the Silverveil teach that?

The absurdity of this thought   standing in my chambers at dawn on the morning I'm going to have my entire body rewritten, worrying about sword grip with breasts   is so profound that I laugh. Once. A single, sharp, unhinged sound that echoes off the stone walls and comes back to me sounding exactly like a man losing his mind.

I put the sword back. It can stay here. A relic. A reminder of the man who held it.

**[No stat change   emotional/atmospheric beat]**

### NPC_D0_003: Ser Caedric   The Bench, One Last Time
#### [Passage: `day0_npc_caedric`]
#### Trigger: Player explores corridors (requires `d2_npc_caedric = true`)

He's on the bench. Of course he's on the bench. The bench is as much a part of Caedric as the rusted sword at his hip. I don't know what the palace would do without Ser Caedric on this bench   it would be like removing a load-bearing wall.

"Prince Edrin." He doesn't stand. We stopped pretending years ago. "Early morning for you."

"Couldn't sleep."

"You haven't slept properly in three days. I can tell by the depth of the circles under your eyes. Day one: mild shadow. Day two: noticeable. Today: architectural."

I sit beside him. The bench is warm   body heat, accumulated over what must be hours. Caedric arrives before dawn and stays until after dusk. It's not laziness. It's commitment. He's chosen his post and he holds it.

"You're going to the tower today." Not a question. Caedric deals in certainties.

I look at him sharply. "How "

"I'm seventy-three years old and I've been sitting on this bench watching this palace for twelve years. Ilara crossed the bridge at dawn in full ceremonial robes. The Queen's door has been closed since first light   no visitors, no ministers. The kitchen is running on crisis pastries and you, Prince Edrin, are walking the corridors at an hour when you're usually still in bed, with the face of a man who is about to do something terrible and irreversible."

He adjusts his sword belt. The leather creaks.

"I served your grandmother. Did I tell you that?"

"Several times."

"Then I'll tell you again, because old men repeat themselves and young princes humor them. Your grandmother took the throne at nineteen. She wasn't ready. She was terrified. She told me once   years later, when we were both old enough for honesty   that on coronation day, she looked in the mirror and saw a girl playing dress-up in her mother's crown. She felt like a fraud for the first five years."

"When did she stop?"

"She didn't. She just got better at ruling while feeling like a fraud. That's the secret, Your Highness. Nobody ever feels ready. Not for the crown, not for the throne, not for..." He pauses. Looks at me. Those bright old eyes seeing everything, as always. "Not for any of it. You just do it, and the doing becomes the being, and one morning you wake up and the person in the mirror isn't a fraud anymore   she's just you."

*She.* He said *she.* Deliberately. Knowingly. This old knight on his bench, with his rusted sword and his institutional memory, knows exactly what I'm walking toward this morning, and he's already adjusted his pronouns.

Something in my chest cracks. Just a little.

"Ser Caedric."

"Your Highness."

"Thank you. For the bench. For all of it."

"I'll be here." He settles back. Crosses his ankles. "I'm always here. That's the stubbornness."

### NPC_D0_004: The Gossiping Maids   Different Pair
#### [Passage: `day0_npc_maids2`]
#### Trigger: Player traverses corridors

Two different handmaidens this time   older, more senior. They're not whispering. They're standing at the junction near the east wing, arms full of linens, speaking in low tones that aren't quite whispers but aren't normal volume either. The tone of people who know something significant is happening and are trying to process it through the lens of clean sheets.

" Mistress Alva ordered the ritual chamber linens. The silver ones. Last used for the King's funeral preparation "

"They're not for a funeral. They're for the Moonspire. I saw them being carried across the bridge this morning."

"The Moonspire? But those linens are for "

They see me. The silence is immediate but different from three days ago   not guilty, not startled. Sympathetic. These women know. Not the specifics, perhaps, but the shape of it. The palace has had three days to sense that something is coming, and the senior staff   the ones who've been here long enough to read the signs   have pieced it together.

The older of the two curtsies. Deep. Formal. The kind of curtsy you give a prince, yes, but also the kind you give someone you might not see again in the same form.

"Your Highness. Safe travels."

*Safe travels.* Not *good morning.* Not *how are you.* Safe travels. As if I'm going somewhere far away. Which I am   farther than any ship could carry me.

"Thank you."

I walk on. Their silence follows me like a veil.

**[Stress +3   the palace knows]**

### NPC_D0_005: The Empty Yard   Aldric's Gift
#### [Passage: `day0_npc_aldric_gift`]
#### Trigger: Player visits Training Yard on Day 0

The training yard is empty. No recruits, no guards, no Aldric. Just the flagstones and the weapon racks and the morning light and the replacement training dummy standing where the one I destroyed yesterday stood   fresh leather, new straw, untouched. Someone repaired the world overnight while I was staring at ceilings.

On the weapon rack, something that wasn't there before. A practice sword   not the standard issue from the armoury. Smaller. Lighter. The grip wrapped in new leather, sized for a narrower hand. The balance point adjusted forward for a shorter reach. Beside it, a piece of parchment.

*Your Highness.*

*I had the armourer make this overnight. The grip is for a smaller hand. The weight is adjusted for a lighter frame. The balance is set for someone approximately five inches shorter.*

*I don't know what's happening today. I know enough.*

*When you're ready to train again, this will be waiting.*

*  Aldric*

He doesn't say what he knows. He doesn't need to. He read the signs   the same signs Caedric read, the same signs the senior maids read, the same signs the entire palace has been reading for three days and his response is not panic, not judgment, not political calculation. His response is to commission a sword.

This man. This impossibly, infuriatingly good man. His prince is about to become someone else and his first thought is *she'll need a weapon that fits.*

I pick up the practice sword. It's too light for my current hands, too small for my current grip. But tomorrow   tomorrow it will be right. Tomorrow these hands will be smaller, and this grip will fit, and Aldric will stand in this yard and teach me to fight all over again in a body I don't know yet.

I set the sword back on the rack. I take the parchment. I fold it and put it in my pocket, next to the crumpled list with every item crossed off and the Veltham letter. Three pieces of paper that describe the architecture of this week: the trap, the intelligence, and the love.

### NPC_D0_006: Marta   The Last Pastry
#### [Passage: `day0_npc_marta3`]
#### Trigger: Player visits Kitchen on Day 0 (requires previous Marta encounters)

She's been baking since midnight. The evidence is everywhere   every surface covered, every rack loaded, the output of a woman who processes anxiety through butter and flour. The kitchen staff move around her the way water moves around a boulder   with respect and without questioning the boulder's right to be there.

She sees me in the doorway and stops. Sets down the rolling pin. Wipes her hands on her apron. She doesn't speak for a moment.

"Sit," she says. Her voice is different. Not softer   Marta doesn't do soft. But the usual declarative force is tempered by something. She points to the stool. My stool. The permanent one.

I sit. She puts a plate in front of me. One pastry. Not cheese   apple, this time. Cinnamon and sugar and thin-sliced fruit in a latticed crust. She hasn't made apple pastries since I was twelve.

"I made your favourite," she says. "The one you used to beg for when you were little. Before you got too princely to beg."

I look at the pastry. I look at Marta. Her eyes are red. She's been crying. Marta   who I have never, in twenty years, seen show anything other than volcanic confidence and wooden-spoon authority   has been crying in her kitchen at midnight while baking pastries from my childhood.

"Marta "

"Eat it. Don't talk. Just eat it."

I eat it. The flavour is a time machine   twelve years old, sitting on this stool (it was taller then, or I was shorter), legs swinging, watching Marta roll pastry and telling her about my day in the breathless, overlapping sentences of a child who hasn't yet learned that adults don't always listen. She always listened. She listened the way she cooked   with full attention, with care, with the understanding that the thing being made deserved respect.

The pastry is perfect. The cinnamon is warm. The apple is sharp. The sugar is sweet.

"Whatever happens today," Marta says, her back to me, stirring the pot that doesn't need stirring, "whatever changes   you come to my kitchen. You hear me? You come and sit on that stool and I will feed you. I don't care what you look like. I don't care what name you carry. You sit on that stool and you are my boy. Always."

"I might not be a boy anymore, Marta."

"Then you'll be my girl. Same stool. Same pastries. Same Marta."

I stand. I walk around the prep table. I hug her. She's solid   barrel-built, flour-dusted, smelling of butter and yeast and the particular warmth of a woman who has fed a palace for thirty years. She hugs me back. Hard. The wooden spoon presses against my shoulder blade. She's still holding it. Of course she is.

"Go," she says, releasing me, turning away so I can't see her face. "Go do what you need to do and come back hungry."

**[Energy +10, Stress -10, Reputation (Staff) +2, Marta bond maxed]**

### NPC_D0_007: Haeth   The Book Between the Pages
#### [Passage: `day0_npc_haeth`]
#### Trigger: Player visits Library on Day 0

The library is empty. Dawn light through the tall windows. The painted constellations on the ceiling turn slowly in the silence. Dust motes drift.

Haeth is at his desk. He's not cataloguing. He's sitting with his hands folded, spectacles on the desk beside him, watching the door. He was waiting for me.

"Your Highness."

"Haeth."

"I assumed you might come. People often return to libraries before significant events. The instinct to seek knowledge persists even when knowledge can no longer change the outcome."

He stands   slowly, joints protesting. He walks to the restricted case. Opens it with his own key. Removes the blue-leather diary.

"I told you there was a letter between pages forty-three and forty-four."

"You did."

"I didn't tell you there was a second item. Between pages sixty-one and sixty-two." He opens the diary. Between the pages   pressed flat, preserved by the dry library air   is a flower. Small, white, star-shaped. Dried but intact.

"Moonflower," he says. "The primary ingredient in the transformation potion. Someone   the anonymous author of this diary, presumably   pressed it here after the Rite. A memento. A marker. The flower that changed them."

He holds it out. The dried moonflower sits on his wrinkled palm, weightless and ancient and impossibly delicate.

"I've been keeping this for forty-three years, waiting for someone who needed it more than the book did."

I take the flower. It's so light it barely registers in my hand. A flower from a ritual performed centuries ago, pressed between the pages of a diary by someone who underwent the same transformation I'm about to undergo, preserved by a librarian who understood that some things are more than historical artefacts.

"Keep it," Haeth says. "Through the Rite and after. A reminder that someone walked this path before you and left a flower behind for the next person."

I close my hand gently around it. The petals are dry and papery and they don't crumble because Haeth has spent forty-three years ensuring this library's climate is perfect for the preservation of fragile things.

"Thank you, Haeth."

"Thank the person who left it. I merely stored it."

He returns to his desk. Sits. Puts on his spectacles. Resumes the posture of a man who has been in this library for forty-three years and intends to remain for forty-three more.

**[Item: Pressed Moonflower, WIS +1, RES +2, Stress -5, Haeth bond maxed]**

### NPC_D0_008: Old Wynn   The Jasmine Survived
#### [Passage: `day0_npc_wynn3`]
#### Trigger: Player visits Gardens on Day 0 (requires previous Wynn encounters)

The jasmine survived.

Wynn has uncovered it   the burlap pulled back, the branches exposed to the morning air. Three branches dead from the frost, cut clean and sealed with wax, exactly as he described. But beneath the dead wood: green. New shoots pushing outward from the core, finding light, reaching for the sky in directions the old branches never went.

Wynn is standing beside it, trowel in hand, looking at the new growth with the quiet satisfaction of a man who bet on life and won.

"Your Highness." He doesn't turn. "The jasmine survived."

"You said it would."

"I said it might. Nature doesn't make promises. It makes attempts." He touches a new shoot   green, tender, pushing outward from where the dead branch was removed. "Different shape now. New directions. But smell it."

I lean close. The scent   jasmine, sweet, heavy, unmistakable. The same scent it's always had. The same scent that fills the garden on summer evenings and drifts through the palace windows.

"Same flower," Wynn says. "Same roots. Same scent. Just wearing a new shape."

He straightens. Looks at me. The sharp old eyes are bright with morning light and something kinder than wisdom.

"The frost was hard. Harder than I expected. But the core held. That's what matters, Your Highness. The core holds. Everything else is just branches."

He hands me a sprig of jasmine   a fresh cutting from the surviving plant, three small white flowers still opening. The scent is immediate and overwhelming, a wave of sweetness that cuts through the morning cold.

"For the tower," he says. "Something alive, to take with you."

### NPC_D0_009: Mistress Alva   Operational Readiness
#### [Passage: `day0_npc_alva3`]
#### Trigger: Player visits Servant Quarters on Day 0

Alva is standing at her desk, not sitting. Her spine is straighter than usual, which I didn't think was physically possible. Her iron-grey hair is pinned with mathematical precision. Her apron is spotless. She radiates the aura of a woman who has been preparing for something significant and has achieved a state of operational perfection.

"Your Highness. I've been expecting you."

"You're always expecting me."

"I'm always expecting everyone. It's how I maintain this household." She folds her hands. "I have a report."

"A report."

"The Moonspire ritual chamber has been prepared. Fresh linens   the silver set. Candles replaced. Temperature regulated via warming enchantments. Recovery supplies staged: heated blankets, fortified broth, clean robes in three sizes because I was not given specific measurements for the post-ritual body and I refuse to be caught unprepared."

She pauses. Adjusts a pencil on her desk by two millimeters.

"The east wing has been alerted that the Queen may require additional support today. Emotional support, not medical   Aldwyn is handling the medical. The kitchen has been instructed to prepare light meals suitable for recovery   broth, bread, soft cheese. Marta has been baking since midnight, which I did not order but have chosen not to countermand because the woman processes trauma through pastry and the results are excellent."

"Alva "

"I'm not finished. The guest wing has been notified that your friend   the bard   will require extended accommodation. I've assigned them the corner room with the garden view because the room has good acoustics and they'll want to play, and playing near a window is better for the instrument and the player's mood. The laundry rotation has been adjusted to accommodate additional linen usage in the Moonspire. The guard rotation has been briefed by Captain Vane   I don't know what he told them specifically, but the east wing and Moonspire guards are all senior personnel, handpicked, trustworthy."

She stops. Looks at me. For one moment   one fraction of a second   the professional mask slips, and I see beneath it the face of a woman who has served this crown for thirty years and is watching the prince she's managed laundry for since he was an infant walk toward something that terrifies her.

"The household is ready, Your Highness. Whatever comes out of that tower   we're ready."

"Alva. You've thought of everything."

"That is my function." She adjusts the pencil again. Same two millimeters. "One more item. I've placed a handkerchief on your desk. Monogrammed. Your initial."

"My initial is about to change."

"I know. That's why I placed it now. So you'd have it. The last one."

I don't know what to say. So I bow   a slight inclination, the kind a prince gives to an equal. Alva's eyes widen fractionally. In thirty years of service, no royal has bowed to her.

"Thank you, Alva. For everything."

"Don't thank me. Come back alive and eat sitting down."

### NPC_D0_010: Apprentice Nara   Preparing the Chamber
#### [Passage: `day0_npc_nara2`]
#### Trigger: Player explores Moonspire Tower before committing to bridge (requires `d1_npc_nara = true`)

She's on the stairs. Carrying candles   an armful of white wax pillars that she's struggling to balance. Her robe keeps catching under her feet. The enchanted music box is clipped to her belt, playing three notes on repeat   a fragment of the sympathetic resonance experiment, still not working right.

"Your   oh!   Your Highness!" She wobbles. A candle slides. She catches it with her chin, which is both impressive and alarming. "I'm setting up the chamber. Ilara wants four hundred and twelve candles. Four hundred and twelve. Not four hundred, not four hundred and twenty   four hundred and TWELVE. Because apparently the magic cares about prime factors."

"Four hundred and twelve isn't prime."

"I KNOW! I told her that! She said it factors into sacred geometries that I'll understand in my sixth year. I'm in my third year. I have three more years of not understanding sacred geometries."

She sets the candles down on a step. Catches her breath. Looks at me.

"You're going through with it." Not a question. She's been in this tower long enough to read the signs.

"I am."

"Can I tell you something? Something I probably shouldn't?"

"Go ahead."

"I was born with my left hand fused   the fingers webbed together. Couldn't grip, couldn't hold. The Silverveil fixed it when I was three. Transformation magic. Not the Rite   a smaller working, just the hand. But the principle is the same."

She holds up her left hand. Five fingers, separate, functional. A faint silvery line runs between the ring and middle fingers   a scar, or a memory of one.

"I don't remember having the webbed hand. I was too young. But my mother says I screamed for an hour after the working, and then I stopped, and I looked at my new hand, and I opened and closed my fingers for the rest of the day because I couldn't believe they moved separately. She says the first thing I gripped was a wooden spoon. Which is probably why I ended up in a tower full of tools and instruments."

She picks up the candles again. Balances them against her chest.

"It's going to hurt. I won't pretend it won't. But on the other side of the hurt, there are fingers that move. There's a hand that grips. There's a you that works in ways you can't imagine yet."

She starts up the stairs. Pauses. Turns back.

"Four hundred and eleven candles are set. You're my last trip. When I place this batch, the chamber is ready. For you."

She climbs. The music box plays its three notes. The candles glow faintly in her arms, anticipating the fire.

**[Stress -5, Silverveil Familiarity +2, Nara Familiarity +2]**

### NPC_D0_011: Mistress Vael   The Potion Is Ready
#### [Passage: `day0_npc_vael2`]
#### Trigger: Player explores Apothecary on Day 0

The Apothecary smells different today. The usual competing scents have been replaced by a single dominant aroma   silver and cold and floral and something underneath that smells like blood but isn't, quite. The moonflower essence.

Vael is standing at her bench. Before her, a silver goblet. Ancient, plain, undecorated. Inside, the potion shifts between colours   silver, deep violet, near-black blue. It catches the light from the single window and refracts it into tiny rainbows on the stone walls.

"It's ready," she says. She doesn't turn around. "Brewed it overnight. Your blood was the last component   Ilara drew it while you were unconscious during your first visit." She pauses. "That sounds alarming. It was a small amount. Standard procedure. The magic needs to know who it's rewriting."

"You could have mentioned the blood-drawing."

"You had enough to worry about. A missing tablespoon of blood was not going to improve your day."

She picks up the goblet. Holds it to the light. The potion ripples   not from movement, but from the magic inside it, which is alive, or something close to alive, vibrating with contained potential.

"This is you," she says. "Your blood, your template, your body's latent potential, combined with moonflower and silverveil silk and three hundred years of accumulated magical knowledge. When you drink it, it won't feel like taking medicine. It'll feel like swallowing a decision."

She sets the goblet back on the bench. Looks at me.

"I've brewed a lot of potions in twenty years. Healing draughts, sleeping tinctures, love philters   don't tell anyone about the love philters   fertility aids, contraceptives, hangover cures. This is the first one I've made that will change someone's entire life in a single swallow."

"Does that scare you?"

"Yes." The bluntness. The honesty. Vael's gift. "But I checked it seven times. The ratios are perfect. The moonflower is peak potency. The blood integration is clean. If this potion doesn't work, it'll be because the magic failed, not because the brewing did and I trust the magic."

She hands me a small cloth pouch.

"Ginger root. Chew it after you drink the potion. It won't reduce the pain, but it'll keep you from vomiting, and vomiting wastes the potion and we'd have to start the brewing over and none of us want that."

"Thank you, Vael."

"Don't thank me. Thank the moonflowers. They died for this." A beat. "That's an alchemy joke. They don't actually die. They're cultivated. Go. You're making the potion nervous."

**[WIS +1, Stress +3 (confronting the reality), Rite Knowledge +2, Item: Ginger Root]**
