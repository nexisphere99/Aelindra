### NPC_D-1_001: The Royal Healer   Master Aldwyn
#### [Passage: `day-1_npc_aldwyn`]
#### Trigger: Player explores corridors before Queen scene

I nearly collide with him at the junction where the east wing corridor meets the central staircase. Master Aldwyn   the Royal Healer   is moving fast for a man of sixty, his robes billowing behind him like he's being chased by his own urgency. He carries a leather satchel that clinks with glass vials, and his face has the particular expression of a physician who has bad news and is trying to outrun it.

"Your Highness " He stops. Adjusts his grip on the satchel. The vials inside clink again, a small dissonant music. "I was coming to find you."

"The Queen?"

"Had a difficult night. The coughing episodes increased   four between midnight and dawn. The third one..." He pauses. Aldwyn is a man who measures his words the way an apothecary measures doses   precisely, with full awareness of their effect. "The third one was productive."

"Productive." I hate medical euphemisms. They dress suffering in clean linen and call it clinical.

"There was blood. More than I'd like. I've adjusted her tincture   stronger anti-haemorrhagic, increased the pain management, added a sleeping draught for tonight." He looks at me. His eyes are tired. Physicians who care about their patients always have tired eyes, and Aldwyn has cared about my mother for fifteen years. "She's refusing to rest, of course. She's been writing correspondence since dawn."

"Of course she has."

"Your Highness... I know you're carrying a great deal. I won't pretend to know what she's asked of you   I know only what falls within my remit, and my remit is her body, not her politics. But I need you to understand: the trajectory is not ambiguous. Four months was my generous estimate. I'm revising it. Three, perhaps. Perhaps less."

Three months. The number drops into my chest like a stone into a well.

"I'm not telling you this to pressure you," he continues, though we both know that's exactly what the information does. "I'm telling you because you have the right to know. Whatever decisions are before you   they have less time than you think."

He adjusts the satchel again. The vials clink. He bows   perfunctory, the bow of a man who has too many patients and too few hours   and continues down the corridor at his urgent clip.

I stand at the junction. Three months. Maybe less. The corridor stretches in both directions   toward the east wing and my mother, toward the central palace and the rest of my life, whatever shape that life takes.

The junction feels metaphorical. I resent it for that.

### NPC_D-1_002: Messenger from Lord Veltham
#### [Passage: `day-1_npc_messenger`]
#### Trigger: Player explores corridors (MORNING or MIDDAY)

A palace runner intercepts me near the stairwell   a lean boy of sixteen whose entire purpose is to carry messages at speed through the palace's labyrinthine corridors. He's breathing hard. He's also terrified, because delivering messages to the prince apparently ranks somewhere between "thrilling" and "potentially fatal" in the servant hierarchy.

"Your Highness   a letter. From Lord Veltham. Delivered to the palace gate this morning, marked urgent."

He holds out a sealed parchment. The seal is green wax   Veltham's sigil, a pair of crossed keys over a wheat sheaf. Lord Veltham is a minor lord from the agricultural provinces. Wealthy enough to matter. Not powerful enough to threaten. A reliable ally of the crown for two generations.

I break the seal. The letter is written in Veltham's hand   careful, formal, slightly trembling at the edges in a way that suggests he wrote it quickly and under stress.

*Your Royal Highness,*

*I write with urgency and in confidence. Lord Ashborne has dispatched envoys to the northern provinces with proposals of alliance. Three houses have received formal visits this week   Thornecrest, Wyld-Marsh, and my own. The proposals are framed as trade agreements, but the substance is political: support for Ashborne's succession claim in exchange for tax concessions, trade privileges, and territorial adjustments.*

*I refused. Thornecrest refused. Wyld-Marsh is considering.*

*I do not know the extent of Ashborne's diplomatic campaign, but the pace suggests urgency. He is moving quickly, as if on a timetable. Whatever your mother's advisors are telling you   it may be more pressing than they know.*

*Your servant in loyalty,*
*Lord Edric Veltham*

I fold the letter. My hands are steady. My stomach is not.

Ashborne isn't just waiting for the Elder Council to rule. He's actively building his coalition. He's preparing for the transition as if it's already decided   as if my mother's death is a scheduling matter and the aftermath is a business opportunity.

Three houses visited in one week. How many more that Veltham doesn't know about?

I need to show this to Mother. Or to her advisors. Or to 

I put the letter in my pocket. Next to the crumpled list with every item crossed off. Two pieces of parchment that together describe the exact shape of the trap I'm standing in.

The runner is still hovering. "Reply, Your Highness?"

"Tell Lord Veltham: received, with gratitude. His loyalty is noted and will be remembered."

The runner nods, bows, and sprints off. I watch him go and think about urgency, about timetables, about a kingdom being divided like property before the owner is even dead.

### NPC_D-1_003: Silverveil Apprentice   Nara
#### [Passage: `day-1_npc_nara`]
#### Trigger: Player explores Moonspire Tower after Ilara scene

The tower's lower levels are where the apprentices live and study. I discover this by taking a wrong turn on the spiral staircase   down instead of up, toward the bridge   and ending up in a corridor lined with doors, each painted a different colour, each radiating a faint warmth that suggests personal enchantment.

One door is open. Inside, a young woman sits cross-legged on the floor surrounded by books, crystals, and what appears to be a partially disassembled music box that is playing itself despite having no visible mechanism. She's nineteen, maybe twenty   dark-skinned, close-cropped hair, wide brown eyes that go even wider when she sees me in her doorway.

"Oh. Oh no. You're the   you're him. The prince. Prince Edrin." She scrambles to her feet. A book falls. A crystal rolls. The music box hiccups and plays three notes backward. "I'm Nara. Apprentice. Third year. I   please don't tell Ilara I was sitting on the floor, she says it's 'energetically irresponsible' but I can't think in chairs "

"I won't tell Ilara."

"Oh thank the Mother." She exhales. Then catches herself. "I mean   welcome to the Moonspire. Are you   do you need something? I can get Ilara, or "

"I'm leaving, actually. I just took the stairs in the wrong direction."

"Oh! Right. Yes. Up for the bridge, down for us. Common mistake. Well   not common, because almost nobody comes here. You're the first visitor in... months? Ilara doesn't encourage socialising. She says it dilutes the magical focus. I think she just doesn't like people."

I almost smile. The girl's nervousness is genuine and completely without agenda   she's not performing, not calculating, not angling for favour. She's a student who's been caught sitting on the floor with a broken music box, and her primary concern is not getting in trouble.

"What are you working on?"

Her face transforms. The nervousness doesn't disappear, but it's instantly subordinated to excitement   the particular incandescent enthusiasm of someone asked about the thing they love.

"Sympathetic resonance enchantment! The idea is that you enchant two objects so they vibrate in harmony   what happens to one happens to the other. The music box is the test case. If I can get it working, you could play a melody on one box and the paired box would play the same melody anywhere in the world. Instant communication through music!"

"That's... remarkable."

"It doesn't work yet." The music box chooses this moment to emit a loud discordant twang and go silent. "See? The sympathetic bond degrades over distance. I can get it to work across a room but not across a building. Ilara says I'm trying to run before I can walk. I say walking is boring."

She seems to remember who she's talking to. The enthusiasm dims, replaced by an awkward formality.

"I'm sorry, Your Highness. You don't want to hear about music boxes. You're here for... important things."

"I'm here because I took the wrong stairs and the music box is considerably more interesting than the things I'm here for."

Something in my voice   the flatness of it, maybe, or the weight beneath the words   makes her look at me more carefully. She's a Silverveil apprentice. She may not know the specifics of what Ilara and I discussed, but she knows the tower, she knows the Rite exists, and she's not stupid.

"The tower looks scary from outside," she says, quietly. "All that white stone and the glowing and the wind on the bridge. But inside it's just... rooms and people and badly enchanted music boxes. It's not as frightening as it looks."

"Is that wisdom or comfort?"

"Both. I specialise in efficiency."

I leave her with her crystals and her broken music box and her earnest, uncomplicated kindness. The stairs wind upward. The tapestries shift. The tower is, as she said, just rooms and people.

It helps to remember that. Slightly.

### NPC_D-1_004: The Apothecary   Mistress Vael
#### [Passage: `day-1_npc_vael`]
#### Trigger: Player explores "Apothecary" in Moonspire Tower

The Apothecary occupies a room on the tower's third level   a circular chamber with walls lined floor to ceiling with shelves of glass bottles, ceramic jars, dried plant bundles, mineral samples, and things I cannot identify and am not certain I want to. The air is thick with competing scents: lavender, something acrid, something sweet enough to make my back teeth ache, and an undertone of alcohol used as a solvent.

Mistress Vael is the Silverveil's alchemist. She's forty-five, red-haired, broad-shouldered, with the kind of face that suggests she has strong opinions and will share them regardless of whether you asked. She's grinding something in a stone mortar with more force than seems necessary.

"Ah. The prince." She doesn't stop grinding. "Ilara said you might wander. She said to answer your questions honestly and send you home before you touch anything."

"I wasn't planning to touch anything."

"Everyone says that. Then they pick up the blue bottle on the third shelf. Don't pick up the blue bottle on the third shelf. It's a concentrated soporific and the fumes alone will put you to sleep for fourteen hours."

I look at the blue bottle. It sits innocently among its neighbours, unremarkable, deeply tempting.

"You brew the potion for the Rite," I say.

"I prepare the base. Moonflower essence, silverveil silk dissolved in a specificity solution, and a personalised blood component. The blood goes in last   it's what tells the magic who you are and what you need to become." She sets down the mortar. Looks at me. "You want to know if it tastes bad."

"I want to know what it does."

"It opens the body to transformation. Think of it as... unlocking a door that's been sealed since birth. Every human body carries the potential for both sexes   the same tissue that becomes a penis in one body becomes a clitoris in another. The same glands, the same structures, just expressed differently. The potion doesn't create something from nothing. It tells your body to express what was always latent."

"Ilara said something similar. That it reveals rather than creates."

"Ilara is philosophically precise. I'm practically precise. The philosophy means it's not an invasion   your body isn't being forced into a foreign shape. It's being told to do something it already knows how to do, just hasn't done. The practice means it's going to hurt like being turned inside out and wrung like a dishcloth, and you'll want to die for about four hours of the six, and then you'll wake up and your hands will be smaller and your hips will be wider and you'll need to learn to piss sitting down."

Her bluntness is   strangely   more comforting than Ilara's precision. There's something reassuring about someone who describes the worst honestly, without padding.

"The previous subjects. Their potions "

"I didn't brew them. My predecessor did   Mistress Thorval, two generations back, for Prince Castien. But the records are thorough. Each potion was tailored to the individual. Each worked. The formula hasn't changed because the magic hasn't changed. Transformation is one of the oldest schools   older than healing, older than divination. The body knows what to do. We just give it permission."

She picks up the mortar again. Resumes grinding.

"If you do this   when you do this   I'll bring the potion to the ritual chamber myself. It'll be warm. It'll taste of iron and flowers and something you can't name. Drink it all. Don't sip. The magic works best when it hits all at once."

"You said 'when,' not 'if.'"

"I've been doing this for twenty years, Your Highness. I know the look. You've already decided. You just haven't told yourself yet."

The grinding continues. I leave the Apothecary smelling of lavender and certainty I don't feel.

**[WIS +2, Stress +3 (honest confrontation with details), Rite Knowledge +1]**

### NPC_D-1_006: Aldric's Second   Sergeant Voss
#### [Passage: `day-1_npc_voss`]
#### Trigger: Player explores Training Yard perimeter (AFTERNOON)

Sergeant Voss is Aldric's second-in-command   a compact, broad woman of thirty with shoulders like a bull and a voice that could strip paint from a wall at fifty paces. She runs the guard training rotations when Aldric is on duty with the royal family. The recruits are terrified of her. The veterans respect her. Both are correct.

She's supervising a group of junior guards running a drill   shield wall formation, the kind of disciplined cooperative work that turns individual soldiers into a unit. She watches with the calculating stillness of a predator assessing prey.

"Your Highness." She doesn't turn from the drill. "Captain Vane mentioned you had a vigorous session today."

"That's one word for it."

"He said the training dummy should be given a state funeral." Still not turning. "I've requisitioned a replacement. The armoury is used to it   we lose three or four a month to recruits who haven't learned that technique matters more than force. Though I suppose princes have different reasons."

"Same reason, usually. Bad day."

Now she turns. Voss's eyes are dark brown, assessing, utterly without deference. She respects rank because rank is structure, but she doesn't genuflect.

"Your Highness. Permission to speak freely."

"Granted."

"The Guard is aware that the palace is under unusual tension. Doubled shifts on the east wing. Silverveil movement on the bridge. The Queen's health   we're not blind, and we're not deaf, and the servant gossip has been... extensive."

"And?"

"And the Guard's loyalty is to the crown. Not to a person   to the crown. Whoever wears it. However they come to wear it. I want you to know that." She holds my gaze. "Whatever changes are coming, the Guard will adapt. We always do."

There's a weight to the word *changes* that tells me Voss knows more than she's saying   or suspects more than she can confirm. The palace is a small world. Secrets don't stay secret. They merely become information that people choose not to acknowledge out loud.

"Thank you, Sergeant."

"Thank me by wrapping those hands properly. I saw the bandage job. It's embarrassing. Come to the barracks dispensary   I'll have the medic do it right."

"It's fine "

"Your Highness. I'm not asking."

I go to the barracks dispensary. The medic rewraps my hands properly   clean linen, salve on the blisters, tight enough to protect without restricting movement. It takes ten minutes and costs me nothing except a mild lecture about grip technique that I probably deserve.

### NPC_D-1_007: Marta's Worry   Kitchen Redux
#### [Passage: `day-1_npc_marta2`]
#### Trigger: Player enters Kitchen (requires `d2_npc_marta = true`)

The kitchen is running at what Marta calls "crisis tempo"   the particular accelerated rhythm that occurs when the Head Cook senses institutional distress and responds by cooking more aggressively. The output is staggering: bread in three varieties, two soups, a roast being turned on the spit by a sweating pot-boy, pastries cooling on wire racks, and what appears to be the beginning of a multi-layered cake of the kind usually reserved for state occasions.

"You're baking a siege supply," I say from the doorway.

Marta wheels around, wooden spoon raised like a mace. She lowers it when she sees me   not entirely.

"I'm baking because this palace is going to need fed, and I've never once seen a problem made worse by pastry." She points the spoon at the stool. It's the same stool from yesterday. I suspect it has been designated My Stool by permanent decree. "Sit. You didn't eat breakfast."

"How do you "

"Your tray came back untouched. The bread, the cheese, the meat   all still there. Even Brin noticed, and Brin notices very little that isn't directly in front of his face."

I sit. She puts food in front of me   bread torn from a warm loaf, a bowl of thick soup that smells of onion and thyme and the kind of slow-cooked patience that can't be rushed. I eat because refusing Marta's food would require more energy than I have.

"The Queen's quarters went through four changes of linen last night," she says, not looking at me, stirring a pot that doesn't need stirring   her thinking gesture, unchanged since yesterday. "Aldwyn ordered fortified broth at midnight. The extra guards on the east wing are rotating on four-hour shifts instead of eight."

She's telling me what the numbers look like. The human cost, measured in laundry and soup and guard schedules. Marta doesn't deal in politics. She deals in the logistics of feeding and maintaining a palace, and the logistics tell a story that's harder to argue with than any legal brief.

"Whatever you're deciding, Your Highness," she says, finally turning to face me, her flour-dusted face set in an expression that's half maternal fury and half something gentler that she'd never admit to, "decide soon. The palace can feel it. When the crown is uncertain, the whole building holds its breath and I can't cook on held breath."

The soup is excellent. The bread is warm. The world is ending and Marta is baking a fortress of carbohydrates against the dark, and somehow this is the most rational response anyone has had to the crisis.

Two days. Everything in my life has a two-day deadline.

### NPC_D-1_009: Librarian Haeth   Quiet Wisdom
#### [Passage: `day-1_npc_haeth`]
#### Trigger: Player interacts with Haeth's desk in Library

Haeth is at his desk. He's always at his desk. I'm beginning to suspect the desk is a part of him   that if you removed Haeth from the Grand Library, the library would dim, the constellations on the ceiling would stop turning, and the books would close themselves in mourning.

He doesn't look up as I approach, but he speaks.

"You read the restricted sources. Elise's key work."

"You know about that."

"I know everything that happens in my library. I know about the key. I know about the diary. I know that Brother Vaelin has been camping in section twelve for thirty-six hours and eating crumbs on my floor, which I find personally offensive." He turns a page of whatever he's cataloguing. "I also know why you were reading what you were reading."

He looks up. Those sharp old eyes   bright as a bird's, missing nothing, forgiving everything.

"I've been librarian here for forty-three years, Your Highness. I was here when your grandmother was queen. I catalogued the Silverveil Compendium myself   I was the one who placed it in the eastern alcove, because the light there is best for reading difficult material and anyone consulting that particular volume would need all the help they could get."

"You placed it there deliberately."

"I place everything deliberately. A librarian's job is not to store books. It's to ensure the right book reaches the right person at the right time. You needed that book. It was where you could find it."

He returns to his cataloguing. Then, without looking up:

"The anonymous diary in the blue leather. The second subject of the Rite. You noticed it was anonymous."

"Yes."

"I've spent thirty years trying to determine the author. I failed. But I found something else   a letter, tucked between the diary's pages when I first acquired the volume, written in a different hand. It was addressed to no one. It said: 'I was afraid of becoming someone else. I became someone I was always meant to be. The fear was real. The becoming was realer.'"

He closes his catalogue. Looks at me over his spectacles.

"That letter is still in the diary, Your Highness. Between pages forty-three and forty-four. If you need it."

I don't go back to the diary. Not today. But I know it's there. Between pages forty-three and forty-four. A letter from someone who walked this road and left a torch burning behind them.

### NPC_D-1_010: Old Wynn   The Gardener's Night
#### [Passage: `day-1_npc_wynn2`]
#### Trigger: Player explores Gardens on Day -1 (requires `d2_npc_wynn = true`)

Wynn is covering the rose beds with burlap. The temperature is dropping   a clear night, which means frost, and frost means vulnerable plants need protecting. He works with the unhurried precision of a man who has been covering rose beds for longer than most of the roses have been alive.

"Your Highness. Walking heavy again."

"You said that yesterday."

"You're still doing it. Heavier tonight." He tucks a corner of burlap around a stem with the tenderness of a parent tucking a blanket. "The tower bridge was used today. I can see it from the herb garden. You crossed it."

"I did."

"I came back."

"Good. Coming back is the important part." He straightens   slowly, spine protesting. "I covered the jasmine last night. Thirty-year-old plant, that one. The frost came harder than I expected, and when I uncovered it this morning, three branches had frozen through. Dead. Black with it."

He moves to the next bed. I follow, because following Wynn through a garden at night is the closest thing to meditation I've found.

"I cut the dead branches. Clean cuts. Sealed the wounds with wax and you know what? Underneath the dead wood, the core was green. Alive. The frost killed the outside but the inside survived. It'll put out new branches in spring. Different directions, different shape, but the same jasmine. Same scent. Same roots."

He looks at me. The same sharp gaze as yesterday   unsentimental, unblinking, the gaze of a man who speaks in plants because plants are more honest than people.

"Whatever the frost is, Your Highness   it kills the outside. The inside survives. That's what roots are for."

He returns to his burlap. The night is cold. The stars are sharp. The garden smells of frozen earth and covered roses and, faintly, of jasmine that survived.

### NPC_D-1_011: Night Guard Perrin   Final Watch
#### [Passage: `day-1_npc_perrin2`]
#### Trigger: Player interacts with door/corridor before sleep (requires `d2_npc_perrin = true`)

The corridor is quiet. Perrin is at his post. He looks up when I open the door   not startled, but attentive, the way a good night guard should be.

"Your Highness."

"Perrin."

"Your friend left a while ago. The one with the lute." A pause. "Sadder music tonight."

"Yes."

We stand in the silence for a moment. Perrin's face is neutral   the professional blankness of a guard on duty   but his eyes have that quality I noticed last night: a genuine attention, unforced, uncalculating. He's not a complex man. He guards a door. He does it well. There's a purity to that.

"I asked you last night about choices you didn't want to make."

"The tannery. I remember."

"What if the choice changes everything? Not just your work or where you live   everything. The person you are. The body you wake up in."

"My grandmother used to say that people aren't their bodies. She said bodies are like clothes   you wear them, they wear out, they change. She was a small woman, my grandmother. Alva is reviewing shift schedules at her desk   a plain oak table in a plain room that serves as the nerve center of palace household operations. She looks up when I enter with the expression of a woman who expected me and has already prepared.

"Your Highness. I have three items."

She doesn't ask how I am. Alva doesn't trade in pleasantries. She trades in information, and she trades it efficiently.

"First: the Ashborne courier who visited the Elder Council yesterday also visited the palace solicitor's office. He delivered a document   my staff couldn't determine the contents, but the solicitor's clerk mentioned the words 'succession challenge' and 'expedited hearing.' The formal challenge may come sooner than your mother expects."

My jaw tightens.

"Second: Archsorceress Ilara requisitioned additional moonflower essence from the palace apothecary stores this morning. The quantity is consistent with ritual preparation. She also requested privacy enchantments for the Moonspire bridge   sound-dampening wards, to be activated at her discretion."

She knows. Alva knows, or has deduced enough to know. She doesn't say it. She doesn't need to.

"Third: the kitchen stores are at seventy-two percent capacity. Marta is baking at crisis tempo, which depletes flour and butter reserves faster than the normal supply rotation can replace them. The last sentence is as close to a joke as Alva gets. I almost miss it.

The east wing linens. She's talking about the Moonspire. About the ritual chamber. About the bed where someone might wake up changed.

She's prepared for it. The whole palace, whether they know the specifics or not, is preparing.
