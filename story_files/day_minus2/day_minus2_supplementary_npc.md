### NPC_D-2_001: The Servant   Brin
#### [Passage: `day-2_npc_brin`]
#### Trigger: Player examines the breakfast tray or interacts with "Servant" prompt

The servant lingers near the door after setting down the tray. He's young   sixteen, maybe seventeen   with sandy hair that sticks up on one side like he slept on it wrong and didn't have time to fix it. His livery is slightly too large. New hire. The palace takes them from common families as a form of patronage   steady work, meals, a roof that doesn't leak.

"Is there anything else you need, Your Highness? More bread? I can get the fresh loaves from the kitchen   Marta just pulled them, they're still steaming "

"The stale bread is fine."

He blinks. Most princes, I imagine, do not prefer stale bread. I could explain the texture argument. I don't.

"You're new," I say instead, because his nervous hovering is going to outlast my patience.

"Third week, Your Highness. I'm Brin. Son of Tomlen the tanner. From the Lower Market district." He says it like he's reciting for an exam. "My mum said to be useful and not drop anything."

"How's that going?"

"I dropped a soup tureen yesterday. In front of Lady Harwick. She made a noise like a horse being stepped on."

I almost smile. Almost. "Lady Harwick makes that noise when her wine is the wrong temperature. Don't take it personally."

He relaxes, marginally. "The guards are saying... there's extra security on the east wing today. Is the Queen "

"The Queen is fine." The lie comes easily. Princes learn to lie before they learn to ride. "She's meeting with advisors. Routine."

He nods, wanting to believe it because the alternative is frightening. I know the feeling.

"Brin."

"Your Highness?"

"The fresh bread. Bring me a loaf for later and tell Marta I said her cheese pastries from last week were excellent."

His face lights up. A small kindness   it costs me nothing and gives him a story to tell in the servants' hall tonight. *The prince remembered the pastries. The prince knows my name.* These things matter to people who are rarely seen.

He leaves with more confidence than he entered.

### NPC_D-2_002: The Half-Written Letter
#### [Passage: `day-2_npc_letter`]
#### Trigger: Player examines the desk / interacts with "Letter" object

I pick up the letter to Rowan. Three weeks old. The ink has crusted in the nib, and when I tap it, a flake of dried black drops onto the parchment.

*"The court is suffocating and I'm considering taking up goat herding as an alternative career "*

That's where I stopped. I remember now. I was trying to be funny. Trying to write something light because Rowan's letters are always light   full of tavern stories and terrible puns and sketches of people they've met on the road. A merchant with a moustache shaped like a seagull. A dog that stole a whole roast chicken from a banquet table. Small, beautiful things.

I wanted to match that energy. I couldn't. Everything I tried to write after the goat herding line turned dark   *Mother is worse. The court is circling. I feel like I'm standing on a trapdoor and someone's hand is on the lever.*

I put the letter down. If Mother is right   if the Rite is the only option   then this letter is a relic from a man who doesn't exist yet. A man who's still deciding.

I fold it. Tuck it into the desk drawer.

If I send it, it'll be the last letter Edrin ever writes.

"Ser Caedric."

### NPC_D-2_005: Head Cook Marta
#### [Passage: `day-2_npc_marta`]
#### Trigger: Player enters the kitchen location

The palace kitchens are a realm unto themselves   a vast, low-ceilinged cavern of stone and iron where three massive hearths blaze day and night, filling the air with heat, smoke, and the complex layered aroma of twelve dishes being prepared simultaneously. Pots hang from ceiling hooks. Herbs dry in bundles above the doorways. A small army of kitchen workers   junior cooks, scullions, pot-boys, pastry apprentices   move in choreographed chaos that would make a military commander weep with envy.

At the center of it all: Marta. Head Cook. Fifty-something. Built like a barrel of ale and twice as formidable. She has flour in her hair, grease on her apron, a wooden spoon in her right hand that functions as both utensil and scepter, and a voice that can be heard through two walls and a courtyard.

"OUT of my way, Joss, or I swear on the Mother's left   Prince Edrin!" Her manner shifts from volcanic to warm in the time it takes her to spot me. "My boy. You ate standing up again, didn't you? I can always tell. Your breakfast tray comes back with the food gone but the chair cold."

"I prefer eating at the window."

"You prefer *not sitting still long enough for your thoughts to catch up.* It's the same thing, and I've been watching you do it since you were nine." She steers me toward a stool near the prep table with the gentle inevitability of a river current. "Sit. Eat. I've got fresh pastries coming out   cheese and herb, the ones you like."

I sit. Resistance is theoretical when Marta is involved.

She puts a plate in front of me   two pastries, golden-brown, steam curling from the latticed top, the smell of melted cheese and rosemary sharp enough to cut through every other scent in the kitchen. They're absurdly good. Marta has been making these since I was a child, and the recipe has not changed because perfection does not require revision.

"You look thin," she says, which is her standard opening regardless of my actual weight.

"You always say that."

"You're always thin. Eat." She watches me bite into the pastry. Her expression settles into satisfaction   the particular look of a cook watching someone enjoy their food, which is the closest thing to religious ecstasy that exists in the culinary profession.

"Marta. Have you heard anything... unusual today? About the east wing?"

Her hands, which were wiping the prep table, slow. She doesn't look up.

"I hear everything in this kitchen, Your Highness. Sound travels through stone. The scullions hear the guards, the guards hear the ministers, the ministers hear the Queen, and all of it ends up in my kitchen before the soup is cold."

"And?"

"And I've been making cheese pastries since dawn. You know what that means."

I do. Marta makes cheese pastries when the palace is scared. She made them the day my father died. She made them during the border skirmish. She made them when the plague hit the lower city three years ago.

"Your mother is the strongest woman I've ever known," Marta says quietly, her back to me, stirring something that doesn't need stirring. "Whatever she's asked of you   she wouldn't ask if there were another way."

I look at her. She looks at the pot. Neither of us says anything else. The kitchen goes on around us   the clatter of pots, the shout of orders, the sizzle of fat in pans. The world is ending and the pastries are perfect and Marta is stirring a pot and somewhere in this equation is a truth about how life continues even when it shouldn't.

"Thank you, Marta."

"Eat the second one. You're thin."

Her form is wrong. Her grip is wrong. Her footwork is wrong. But the intensity is right. She's hitting the dummy like it said something about her mother.

She sees me and freezes. The quarterstaff clatters against the dummy's wooden head one final time, a dying echo.

"Y-Your Highness. I didn't   I wasn't expecting "

"At ease, recruit." I pick up the quarterstaff she's dropped. "You're overextending on your backswing. You're opening up your left side every time you commit to a high strike."

She blinks. "You... know quarterstaff?"

"Aldric made me learn every weapon in the yard before he let me specialise. His philosophy is that a swordsman who doesn't understand a spear doesn't understand reach, and a man who doesn't understand reach doesn't understand combat."

I demonstrate the correction   shorter backswing, elbow tucked, weight on the balls of the feet rather than the heels. She watches with the focused intensity of someone who has been teaching herself from observation and is hearing actual instruction for the first time.

"Tamsin, Your Highness. From the northern garrison. I transferred three weeks ago."

"Three weeks and nobody's corrected your form?"

"The senior guards say recruits learn by doing and by getting hit."

"That's half right. You learn by getting hit *after* someone explains what you're doing wrong. Otherwise you just learn how to get hit."

I work with her for ten minutes. It's the first time today that my mind has been fully in my body   not in the Queen's chambers, not in the library, not circling the drain of the Rite. Just here. Hands on a staff. Feet on stone. The simple physics of force and leverage.

"Thank you, Your Highness." Tamsin's form is marginally better. Marginally is a start.

"Practice the short backswing. Fifty repetitions, morning and night. You'll feel the difference in a week."

"Yes, Your Highness."

I set the staff down. The brief clarity is already fading, the dread seeping back in like water through cracks. But for ten minutes, I was just a man who knew something useful and shared it.

She leads me to the restricted case. It's tall, iron-framed, glass-panelled. Inside, among other ancient documents, sit two items: a small leather-bound diary, cover cracked with age, and a thick manuscript bound in green cloth.

Elise unlocks the case. Her hands are steady despite her nerves   a librarian's hands, precise and careful.

The diary is short. Most of it is water-damaged and illegible. But a few passages survive:

*I was nobody before and nobody after. That is the secret they do not tell you   the Rite does not care who you are. It only cares what you are willing to become. I was willing. I am still deciding if that was courage or surrender.*

*My body is a country I am still learning to navigate. There are borders I did not expect. Territories that surprise me. I map myself daily and the map is never finished.*

I close the diary. My hands are shaking again.

Vesper's treatise is dense, theoretical, and largely incomprehensible without a Silverveil education. But one passage stands out:

*The Rite of Becoming does not create. It reveals. The body holds all possibilities; the Rite merely selects one and makes it dominant. This is why consent is required   the subject must agree to become what they already, in some fundamental sense, are.*

I don't know what to do with that. I put both books back. Elise locks the case.

"Thank you, Elise."

"Your Highness." She pushes her spectacles up. "If you need anything else   anything at all   I'm usually shelving in section twelve. The poetry section. Nobody goes there."

"Noted."

I leave her standing by the case, fingers on the iron frame, watching me go with an expression I can't read.

### NPC_D-2_011: The Gardener   Old Wynn
#### [Passage: `day-2_npc_wynn`]
#### Trigger: Player explores gardens before sitting on the bench

Old Wynn has been the palace gardener for longer than anyone can remember. He predates my mother's reign, my grandmother's reign, possibly the castle itself. He's a small, bent man who smells of soil and speaks to his plants with more affection than he shows to humans. I've never seen him indoors. I'm not convinced he *can* go indoors   like a tree that would wither without sunlight.

He's pruning the winter roses when I find him. His shears click with the metronomic precision of a heartbeat.

"Your Highness." He doesn't look up. "You're walking heavy tonight. I can hear you from the herb beds."

"Am I?"

"Mmm. Heel-striking. You usually land on the balls of your feet   training habit. Tonight you're stomping. Something on your mind."

"Nothing that concerns rose pruning."

"Everything concerns rose pruning, Your Highness." Click. Click. A dead branch falls. "Roses are the most honest plants in the garden. They grow where they're planted, bloom when it's time, and die when they're done. No pretence. No politics. Just... becoming what they are."

He pauses. Looks at the bush   a winter rose, deep crimson, still holding blooms despite the cold.

"This one nearly died last autumn. Root rot. I had to cut it back to almost nothing   just a stump and two canes. The other gardeners said I should pull it out, plant something new. But I know this variety. Deep roots. Patient. I cut away everything that was dying and let it decide for itself whether to grow back."

He touches a bloom. It nods under his calloused finger.

"It grew back. Different shape. New canes going directions I didn't expect. But the flowers are the same. Same colour, same scent. Same rose. Just... wearing a new body."

He looks at me for the first time. His eyes are sharp   the bright, unsentimental gaze of a man who has spent sixty years watching things die and grow and die and grow.

"Good evening, Your Highness."

He turns back to his pruning. Click. Click.

I stand in the garden and listen to the shears and the fountain and the jasmine unfolding and I think about roots and roses and the things that survive the cutting.

"I wanted to walk."

She knows. Of course she knows. Alva knows the location of every person in this palace at any given moment. She's a human panopticon.

"Your breakfast was returned with the bread eaten and the cheese eaten and the meat eaten and the chair cold. You ate standing."

"I did."

"Your mother ate lying down. She's been doing that more often. Her appetite is diminishing. The healers have shifted to fortified broths." Alva's voice is clinical. She reports facts the way a surgeon reports vital signs. "I've increased the laundry rotation for the east wing. The Queen's linens need changing more frequently now."

I don't want to know this. I need to know this. These are the details that the ministers don't mention and the healers euphemise   the physical reality of a dying woman's daily life, measured in linens and broths and the declining angle of a pillow stack.

"Alva. Is there anything she needs?"

"She needs a miracle or a successor. I can provide neither. I can provide clean sheets, hot broth, and a palace that runs smoothly enough that she can focus her remaining energy on governance rather than domestic logistics." A pause. "I can also provide information, should you require it."

"I oversee eighty-seven staff. They move through every room in this palace. They see things. Hear things. The east wing guards are on double rotation   that I ordered, at the Queen's request. The Silverveil bridge was crossed by Archsorceress Ilara at dawn   my staff reported it. Lord Ashborne's household sent a courier to the Elder Council this morning   one of my laundresses is married to a Council clerk."

She looks at me. There's no warmth in her eyes, but there's something else   the fierce, unsentimental protectiveness of a woman who has served the crown for thirty years and considers its survival a personal responsibility.

"The household is loyal, Your Highness. Whatever comes."

The same words Keller used. Different voice, same promise. The palace is circling its wagons, and they're doing it quietly, without being asked, because that's what loyalty looks like when it's real.

"Thank you, Alva."

"Don't thank me. Eat sitting down. You'll develop acid reflux."

I open the door to get air   the room is warm from the rebuilt fire and I can't breathe   and nearly walk into the night guard. Perrin. Young. Earnest. The kind of guard who takes the night shift seriously, which means he stands at rigid attention for eight hours even though no one is watching.

He nods. Stands aside. I lean against the doorframe and breathe the corridor air   cooler, stone-scented, with a faint draft from the stairwell.

"Quiet night," I say, because the alternative is going back inside and staring at the ceiling.

"Yes, Your Highness. Very quiet." He hesitates. "Except   there was something. Around midnight. Music. From the guest wing."

"That's Rowan. A friend."

"Ah. Good music. Sad, though."

"Yes."

We stand in comfortable silence for a moment. Perrin is perhaps the least complicated person I've interacted with today   a guard on a post, doing his job, noticing music in the dark. There's something restful about simplicity.

"Perrin. Have you ever had to make a choice you didn't want to make?"

He thinks about it. Actually thinks, which I appreciate   I'm so tired of reflexive answers and political calculations.

The new shape of things. I file that beside *vaelthin* and *one who has walked between* and *the same rose in a new body* and all the other phrases that are collecting in my chest like stones in a cairn.

"Good night, Perrin."

"Good night, Your Highness."

I close the door. The ceiling is waiting.
