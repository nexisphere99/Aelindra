### NPC_D1_001: The Items You Carried
#### [Passage: `day1_npc_items`]
#### Trigger: Player examines "Bedside Table" — items from Day 0 inventory

On the table beside the bed: the things I carried to the tower. Someone — Ilara, probably, or one of the sorceresses — placed them here while I was unconscious. Arranged neatly, as if they matter, because they do.

The pressed moonflower. Haeth's gift. I pick it up. It's as light as I remember — lighter, even, because my fingers are smaller and the contrast makes the flower seem even more impossibly delicate. A flower from another transformation, another body, another person who lay on that altar and woke up different. I hold it against my palm. The petals don't crumble. They survived the night. So did I.

The jasmine sprig. Wynn's cutting. The flowers have closed — jasmine closes during the day and opens at night, which I know because Wynn told me once and I remember everything Wynn tells me about plants. The scent is still there. Faint. Sweet. The same scent. I bring it to my nose and the smell triggers something — a memory of the garden at dusk, the fountain, the first stars. A memory that belongs to the person I was. A memory that's still mine.

Aldric's letter. Folded. I open it and read it again. *The grip is for a smaller hand. The weight is adjusted for a lighter frame.* My eyes blur. The handwriting hasn't changed. Aldric's precise, military script. The words were for a person who didn't exist when he wrote them — a person he couldn't see, couldn't imagine, but prepared for anyway. That's what love looks like when it's expressed in leather and steel.

The ginger root. Vael's practicality. I didn't need it — the potion stayed down. But she thought of it. She thought of vomiting, and then she thought of how to prevent it, and then she gave me the solution wrapped in a cloth pouch with no ceremony and no sentiment. Vael doesn't do sentiment. She does chemistry. It amounts to the same thing.

The monogrammed handkerchief. Alva's last gift. The initial — E — embroidered in gold thread. The wrong initial, now. Or the old initial. Or the initial that connects this body to the body that carried it yesterday, a thread of thread.

I spread them on the table. Five items. Five people who cared enough to give me something to carry through the Rite. Five anchors to a world that still exists beyond these four walls.

**[RES +2, Stress -5 — connection to the world]**

### NPC_D1_002: The Nightgown
#### [Passage: `day1_npc_nightgown`]
#### Trigger: Player examines "Nightgown" — what am I wearing?

Someone dressed me while I was unconscious. That thought arrives late — hours after waking, when the initial shock has subsided enough for secondary details to register. Someone removed my clothes — the jerkin, the shirt, the trousers, the smallclothes — from a body that was in the process of being rewritten, and replaced them with this nightgown. White linen. Thin. Fitted to a body that didn't fully exist when it was put on.

The nightgown is Silverveil-made — the fabric has that faint enchanted quality, slightly warmer than it should be, slightly softer than any linen I've ever felt. It has a drawstring at the neckline. Whoever put it on me pulled the drawstring loose, probably because my chest was still changing and a tight neckline would have been painful.

The breast band was not on when I woke. The breasts were loose under the nightgown. Someone made the decision that a breast band was unnecessary for an unconscious woman whose breast tissue was still developing. Someone handled these breasts while I wasn't aware of it — clinically, for medical purposes, as part of post-operative care. But someone touched them. Someone saw them before I did.

The thought is not violating, exactly. Ilara is a healer. The sorceresses are medical professionals. But the intimacy of it — the fact that my new body was known by others before it was known by me — adds to the fundamental off-balance feeling that defines this day.

Where are my old clothes? I look around. Not in the room. Someone removed them. The jerkin with the reinforced shoulders. The boots, broken in perfectly. The sword belt. Gone. Probably folded and stored somewhere, relics of a body that no longer fits them, artifacts of a person who walked into this tower yesterday and didn't walk out.

### NPC_D1_003: The Queen's Message
#### [Passage: `day1_npc_queen_message`]
#### Trigger: Available in EVENING — a handmaiden knocks with a sealed note

A knock at the door. Not Rowan's knock — softer, more formal. I consider not answering. The door opens a crack — a handmaiden, young, wearing Silverveil grey rather than palace livery. She doesn't enter. She extends a hand through the gap. A sealed parchment. The royal seal — silver stag, crescent moon.

"From the Queen, my lady."

My lady. The first time anyone has addressed me with a feminine honorific. The words hit my eardrums and detonate somewhere behind my sternum. I take the note. The handmaiden curtsies — I see her knees bend through the gap — and retreats.

I break the seal. Mother's handwriting — the elegant, controlled script of a woman who was taught penmanship as a form of statecraft.

*Rest. I'll come to you tomorrow. I love you.*

Seven words. Eight, if you count contractions honestly. And every one of them is a blade and a bandage simultaneously.

*Rest.* Because she knows I won't. Because she knows I'm lying in this room cataloguing every wrong thing about this body and rest is the last thing I'm capable of. The instruction is not advice — it's a mother's magic, the kind that doesn't work but is cast anyway because the alternative is silence.

*I'll come to you tomorrow.* Not today. Today she's giving me the gift of not being seen. Today she knows that being looked at by the woman who asked me to do this would be too much. She's managing me. She's always been managing me. She's doing it from a sickbed with blood in her lungs and she's still better at it than anyone else alive.

*I love you.* Three words that don't change anything and change everything. Three words that are true regardless of the body they're delivered to, regardless of the name attached to that body, regardless of the jaw shape and the chest size and the presence or absence of a cock.

I crumple the note. Throw it at the wall. Watch it bounce off the white stone and land on the floor, a small wadded ball of parchment and maternal love.

Then I pick it up. Smooth it. Read it again. Put it under the pillow.

### NPC_D1_004: Aldric's Voice Through the Door
#### [Passage: `day1_npc_aldric_door`]
#### Trigger: Player interacts with "The Door — sounds from outside" (AFTERNOON)

I hear him before I recognise him. Boots on stone — heavy, even, the cadence of a man who has never been in a hurry. The specific footstep pattern of Sir Aldric Vane, which I have been hearing since I was fourteen and which my brain identifies with the speed and certainty of a body recognising its own heartbeat.

He stops outside my door. I know it's him the way I know the ceiling crack and the morning cold — through accumulated data, the slow geological formation of familiarity.

He doesn't knock.

He stands outside the door for what must be three minutes. I can hear him breathing — the steady, measured breathing of a man who controls his respiration the way he controls his sword arm: deliberately, with discipline, as a matter of professional practice.

Then his voice. Low. Through the oak.

"Your Highness."

Two words. The same two words he's said to me a thousand times — in the training yard, in the corridor, at the palace gate. The same intonation, the same respectful formality. He hasn't changed the address. He hasn't adjusted the register. He's calling me *Your Highness* the way he always has, because the rank hasn't changed even if the person holding it has, and Aldric's loyalty is to the title and the person and neither requires a specific set of chromosomes.

"I'm told you're resting." His voice through the door. I'm standing on the other side. My hand is on the wood. I can feel the vibration of his voice through the oak. "I won't disturb you. I only wanted you to know — the practice sword is on the yard rack. It'll be there when you're ready. No rush."

A pause. The breathing.

"You're still my prince." Another pause. He corrects himself — not because someone told him to, but because he's working it out in real time, standing in a corridor talking to a door. "My princess. My... you're still mine to protect. Whatever you need. I'm down the corridor."

His boots. Walking away. Heavy, even, certain. The sound fades.

I stand with my hand on the door. The wood is warm where I've been pressing. My hand — this new hand, slender and small — flat against the oak, and on the other side, three minutes ago, Aldric's hand might have been doing the same thing.

He called me his. He stumbled on the word — prince to princess, the linguistic adjustment lagging behind the emotional one — and the stumble was the most genuine thing I've heard today because it meant he was trying, in real time, to meet me where I am, and where I am is a place he's never been and can't fully understand and he's coming anyway.

**[Aldric Affinity +3, RES +2, Stress -5]**

### NPC_D1_005: Apprentice Nara — Checking In
#### [Passage: `day1_npc_nara3`]
#### Trigger: Player hears "footsteps and music box outside" or explores corridor (requires previous Nara)

The music box. Three notes, repeating. The sympathetic resonance experiment, still broken, still playing its fragment of melody on a loop. I hear it through the door — and then a soft knock, tentative, the knock of someone who isn't sure they should be here.

"It's Nara. You probably don't want visitors but I brought — um — Ilara asked me to bring fresh candles and I also brought tea because tea helps everything, my grandmother says, and she survived three plagues and a bad marriage so she'd know."

I open the door. Nara is there — holding a tray with a teapot, two cups, and a small plate of biscuits. The music box is clipped to her belt, playing its three-note fragment. She's looking at the floor. At the wall. At the tray. At everything except me.

"You can look," I say.

She looks. Her brown eyes are wide, taking me in — the face, the body, the shirt-and-trousers that fit wrong, the hair that's everywhere. Her expression does something complicated — surprise, adjustment, curiosity, sympathy — and lands on warmth.

"You look like a girl who could use tea." She holds out the tray. "Also biscuits. They're terrible. I baked them myself and I'm a much better enchanter than baker. But they're warm and the gesture matters more than the flavour, that's what my grandmother says."

I take the tray. The biscuits are, as advertised, terrible — dry, slightly burnt, crumbling into sad fragments. The tea is excellent. Silverveil blend, something herbal with a calming enchantment that I can feel smoothing the jagged edges of my nervous system like a warm hand on a rough surface.

"Does the music box work yet?"

"No." She brightens, the way she always does when asked about her work. "But I've narrowed the degradation problem to the sympathetic frequency. The bond holds across a room but the frequency drifts over distance — like two tuning forks that start in harmony and one of them slowly goes flat. I need to stabilise the base frequency. Ilara says I'm overthinking it. I say she's underthinking it."

She talks about music boxes and sympathetic resonance and the magical properties of moonstone for frequency anchoring. I listen. Not because I understand the magical theory but because the sound of someone talking enthusiastically about something that has nothing to do with my body or my transformation or my future is the most restful sound I've heard all day.

"Can I ask you something?" she says, when the tea is half gone. "You don't have to answer."

"Go ahead."

"When I had my hand fixed — the webbed fingers — I couldn't stop opening and closing my new hand. For days. Just... watching the fingers separate. Because they'd never done that before and I couldn't believe they could. Does it feel like that? New things doing new things?"

I consider this. My hand. The slender fingers. The smooth knuckles. I open and close my fist — the motion smooth, precise, the fingers spreading and curling with an ease that seems impossible for structures that are twelve hours old.

"Yes," I say. "Exactly like that. Except everything."

She nods. She doesn't say anything wise or comforting. She just nods, and takes a terrible biscuit, and bites into it with a crunch, and the three-note music box plays on.

**[Stress -8, Silverveil Familiarity +2, Nara Familiarity +3]**

### NPC_D1_006: Mistress Vael — Medical Follow-Up
#### [Passage: `day1_npc_vael3`]
#### Trigger: Player explores "Apothecary" or Vael visits (AFTERNOON)

A knock. Purposeful. Three raps, no pause.

"Open the door. I have your prescriptions and I'm not standing in the corridor."

Vael. She enters before I've fully opened the door — a professional woman with a professional schedule and no time for reluctant patients.

She carries a wooden case. Inside: four glass vials, labelled in precise handwriting.

"Blue vial. Pain management. Take one spoonful at morning and night. The bone aches will last three to four days — your skeleton is still settling into its new configuration.

"Green vial. Hormonal stabiliser. One spoonful at morning. Your oestrogen is spiking — normal, expected, but the emotional turbulence can be significant. This smooths the peaks.

"Clear vial. Sensitivity reducer. Apply topically to the breasts and... other areas if the nerve response is overwhelming. Which it will be. For the first week, everything down there is going to feel like touching a live wire. The reducer brings it down to a manageable level.

"Amber vial. Sleep aid. Two drops on the tongue before bed. You need to sleep. Real sleep, not the anxious half-consciousness of someone lying awake cataloguing everything they've lost."

She closes the case. Looks at me. Her gaze is assessing — not the warm assessment of a friend but the hard assessment of a medical professional evaluating a patient.

"Your posture is compensating. You're hunching forward to hide the breasts. Stop. You'll develop back problems within a week. Stand straight. Let them exist."

"Easy for you to say."

"I've had breasts for forty-five years. They're not a burden — they're a feature. You'll adjust." She softens, fractionally. "The body is working. I checked the potion's residual markers this morning — the transformation is complete, stable, no anomalies. Whatever discomfort you're feeling is psychological, not physical. Which doesn't make it less real — but it means the timeline is yours. The body will wait for you to catch up."

She leaves the case on the table, nods once, and walks out. At the door she pauses.

"Eat something. The hormonal shifts burn calories. You'll be hungry."

She's right. I'm starving. The broth wasn't enough. The biscuits — Nara's terrible biscuits — were barely food. My metabolism, apparently, has been reset along with everything else, and it's demanding fuel with the urgency of a furnace newly lit.

### NPC_D1_007: Sounds Through the Window
#### [Passage: `day1_npc_window_sounds`]
#### Trigger: Player examines "Window" at any time

The window is narrow — an arrow-slit widened to a hand's breadth by some renovation. I can't see much through it. A slice of sky. The top of the bridge railing. The far edge of the palace wall.

But I can hear.

The palace is continuing without me. Through the window, sounds drift up from the world below: the clang of the blacksmith's hammer (distant, rhythmic, the heartbeat of industry). A guard calling shift change. Horses in the courtyard. The creak of the merchant gate. Someone laughing — a woman, bright and high, the kind of unselfconscious laughter that happens in kitchens and gardens and places where people aren't locked in towers processing the loss of their entire physical identity.

Children playing. Somewhere near the palace walls, the shrieks and clamour of children who are running and chasing and being gloriously unaware that the prince who lived in the palace above them is now a princess who doesn't know how to walk without her hips swaying.

A dog barking. Someone selling something in the market — the voice carries, *fresh bread, fresh bread, get your bread,* the sing-song of a vendor who has said these words ten thousand times and will say them ten thousand more.

The world is going on. The same world, the same sounds, the same bread and laughter and hammering and children. Nothing has changed out there. The sun rose. The market opened. The guards changed shift. The baker baked. Nobody in that world below knows that Prince Edrin is gone and a woman called Aelindra is standing at a narrow window, listening to the sounds of a life that used to be hers, in a body that isn't.

The wind carries the scent of the garden — jasmine, faint, sweet. Wynn's jasmine. The one that survived the frost.

Same roots. Same scent. New branches.

**[RES +1, Stress -3 — the world continues, and that is both painful and comforting]**

### NPC_D1_008: The Body — Unprompted Discoveries
#### [Passage: `day1_npc_body_discoveries`]
#### Trigger: Available throughout the day — "Examine yourself" interaction always present

The discoveries don't come all at once. They come in waves, across the day, each one a small earthquake in the ongoing seismic event of living in this body.

**Hair.** I keep finding it everywhere. On the pillow, across my shoulders, falling in front of my eyes. It's heavier than it should be — thicker, denser, with a wave pattern that I didn't have before. When I push it behind my ears, it stays briefly, then slides free. I need something to hold it back. I don't have anything. I twist it into a knot at the back of my head. It holds. The motion is instinctive — a gesture I've seen women make a thousand times and never thought about.

**My voice.** Every time I speak, it surprises me. The pitch is wrong — higher, softer, with less resonance. The words are mine but the instrument producing them is a stranger's. I test it: humming, then singing a line from one of Rowan's songs. The melody is the same. The timbre is completely different. A woman's voice singing a man's song in a woman's body in a tower room at noon.

**Walking.** I practice. Four paces, turn, four paces back. The sway doesn't stop. The hips move. The body moves the way it moves. But by the tenth lap, the stumbling is gone. By the twentieth, the motion is almost fluid. The body learns faster than the mind. It's already adjusting, already finding its own efficient gait, and the mind is just along for the ride.

**Sitting.** My thighs press together when I sit. The vulva is between them — always, constantly, a warm presence that I'm aware of the way you're aware of your heartbeat when someone tells you to listen for it. If I cross my legs (I try it — one knee over the other, the feminine cross, the legs-together default), the sensation changes. Pressure on the mound. A repositioning of the folds. I uncross them immediately.

**Crying.** I don't cry on Day 1. But late in the afternoon, standing at the window, listening to the world go on without me, my eyes fill. The tears don't fall. They sit on the lower lids like water on a leaf, gathering weight, threatening. I blink them back. The tears are the same — salt water, the same composition, the same mechanism. Even the tear ducts survived the rewrite. The body changed everything except the capacity to grieve.

### NPC_D1_009: A Meal Tray — From Marta
#### [Passage: `day1_npc_marta_tray`]
#### Trigger: EVENING — a tray arrives at the door

A tray appears at my door. Not brought by a servant — just there, set on the floor outside, when I open the door after Rowan leaves. A palace kitchen tray, covered with a clean cloth.

Under the cloth: soup (thick, onion and thyme, the same recipe Marta has been making since I was nine). Bread (warm, fresh, the good kind). Two cheese pastries (golden, rosemary). An apple pastry (cinnamon, sugar, the childhood recipe). And a folded note.

*Eat everything. You're thin.*
*Same stool. Same pastries. Same Marta.*
*— M.*

She's not allowed in the tower. The Silverveil restrict access during post-ritual recovery. So she sent a tray. Through channels — probably Alva arranged the logistics, probably Brin carried it to the bridge, probably a Silverveil apprentice brought it the rest of the way.

The food is perfect. It tastes the same. Everything Marta makes tastes the same regardless of the body eating it, because Marta's cooking transcends the merely physical and operates on the level of the soul.

I eat everything. I'm still hungry afterward, because Vael was right about the metabolism. But the hunger is manageable and the soup is warm in my stomach and the note is under my pillow beside the Queen's letter and for the first time today I feel something that is not horror or grief or clinical detachment.

I feel fed. Which is Marta's magic, and it is the most powerful magic in this tower.

**[Energy +15, Stress -8, Reputation (Staff) +1]**
